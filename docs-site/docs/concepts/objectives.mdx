---
title: "Objectives"
description: "Durable, checked-in planning records for work that outlives a single agent session."
---

An **Objective** is a durable, checked-in narrative record for a unit of planned work
that spans multiple sessions, branches, or pull requests. It states what the work is
for, what done means, what the current plan is, and what has actually happened — as
plain Markdown committed to your repository.

## The problem it solves

A coding agent's context lifetime is the session. Real work isn't: a migration, a
feature with a dependency chain, a hardening effort — these run for days or weeks across
many sessions and branches. Without a durable carrier of intent, every new session
starts cold, re-derives the plan from the diff, and drifts. That drift is where slop
comes from: output with no durable intent behind it and no memory of why.

Objectives are the boundary. The record owns the *why* and the *what's left*; each agent
session picks it up, does a slice, and writes back what happened. A fresh session weeks
later starts from the accumulated narrative, not an empty window.

Two properties make this work:

- **Git-native.** Records are ordinary files in your repository — no hidden database, no
  service, no separate account. They travel with clones, they diff, and changes to them
  are reviewed in pull requests like any other change.
- **Shared between humans and agents.** The same record your agent maintains is the one
  you read and edit. There is no separate dashboard to fall out of sync with reality.

## The record

Each Objective lives in a directory under `.ns/objectives/`, named by a durable slug:

<Files>
  <Folder name=".ns/objectives" defaultOpen>
    <Folder name="api-typed-handlers" defaultOpen>
      <File name="objective.md" />
      <File name="roadmap.md" />
      <Folder name="updates">
        <File name="20260705T114219Z-first-slice-landed.md" />
      </Folder>
      <File name="closed.md" />
    </Folder>
  </Folder>
</Files>

- **`objective.md`** — the durable statement: a thesis for why the work matters, scope
  and non-goals, completion criteria, and assumptions and risks. This is the part that
  changes rarely and keeps every session pointed at the same destination.
- **`roadmap.md`** — the current plan as semantic steps under `## Work` (with a
  `## Parked` section for deferred items). Rows carry one of three statuses: `[ ]` open,
  `[~]` in progress, `[x]` done. Steps are units of meaning, not time estimates.
- **`updates/`** — an append-only series of dated updates. Each records what happened,
  how it impacts the Objective, and any follow-ups. This is the narrative memory that
  future sessions load.
- **`closed.md`** — a marker whose existence means the Objective is closed. Closing
  never deletes history; the record stays in the repository.

The directory name is the Objective's durable identity. Titles and prose can be
reworded freely; the slug persists.

## The lifecycle

Objectives move through a simple loop, driven by skills that `ns init` installs into
your harness (Claude Code, Codex, or Pi):

1. **Create** — `objective-create` scaffolds a new record from a statement of intent.
2. **Advance** — `objective-next` reads the record, reconciles it against what has
   actually landed, and recommends the next useful slice of work.
3. **Update** — `objective-update` records progress after a work session or merged PR:
   a dated update plus roadmap status changes.
4. **Close** — `objective-close` records the closure rationale and writes the closed
   marker when completion criteria are met.

Alongside the skills, the [`ns objective` CLI](/docs/tools/objective) is the
deterministic surface for humans and scripts: list records, show one with its details,
validate structure, and archive completed records out of the active set.

Because everything is Markdown, you can also just edit the files. The skills exist to
keep records consistent and evidence-linked, not to gatekeep them.

## What Objectives are not

- **Not an issue tracker.** An Objective is a narrative for one coherent unit of work,
  not a queue of tickets. It records intent and evidence, not assignees and due dates.
- **Not a status dashboard.** Progress lives in prose updates linked to real changes,
  not burndown metrics.
- **Not hidden state.** There is no server and no sync. If it isn't in your repository,
  it doesn't exist.
